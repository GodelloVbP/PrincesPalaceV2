Six Opus hunters against six slices of the tree: combat; run/dungeon/economy/progression;
UiKit/stage/audio/party; the Core->Domain seam (main tree, no worktree); tools/; and a
follow-up specifically on speed buffs and Reach. One integrator gated all of it into main.

## (a) Method

Each hunter read its slice adversarially rather than for style: pick a stated contract --
a comment, a test name, a doc line -- and try to make it lie. Where one did, the hunter wrote
the failing test FIRST, against the contract, before touching the fix, then fixed the
mechanism the test named rather than papering over the symptom it happened to catch. One
commit per bug, so `git log` reads as a list of what was wrong rather than a squashed diff.
Every hunter ran in its own git worktree; a worktree's Unity TestRunner is `[D]`-suffixed
(dotnet host, no scenes, no `-nographics` fixtures), so this is a `tools/test.ps1`-class hunt
by construction -- nothing that needs a real Editor or a built scene got covered here, and the
gate below exists precisely to backfill that gap once. One integrator merged all six branches
into `reward-tracks` on main, ran `-BuildContent -BuildScenes` once at the end
(`0184be2e`), and proved two things a green dotnet-only suite cannot: the content stamp still
describes its inputs, and the five rebuilt scenes are geometry-identical to what was already
committed -- every `m_AnchorMin`/`m_AnchorMax`/`m_AnchoredPosition`/`m_SizeDelta`/`m_Pivot`/
`m_LocalScale`/`m_Text`/`m_FontSize`/`m_Color` line compares as an identical multiset against
HEAD (20703 lines in Fight, 17786 Hub, 23423 Map, 6767 Talents, 1838 MainMenu), so the
448873-insertion/448873-deletion scene diff is fileID reassignment only, not a layout
regression. Final gate: **EditMode 3099 passed, 1 skipped; PlayMode 793 passed, 27 skipped;
zero failures either side.**

## (f) Process notes

**The `%TEMP%` log collision was fixed twice, independently, in two different worktrees, before
either saw the other.** `73bd1466` keyed every `tools/` scratch file by the project ROOT (an
MD5 hash of the checkout path, via a new `tools/temp_paths.ps1`) on the theory that two
worktrees are the two things that actually differ on disk. Three minutes later, `325ed84c`
(different worktree, same night) keyed `test.ps1`'s dotnet transcript and `graphics_tests.ps1`'s
results/log by `$PID` instead, on the theory that the *process* is the thing that is actually
distinct -- two sessions sharing one working tree are just as collision-prone as two separate
worktrees, and a project-root key does nothing for that case. `tools/temp_paths.ps1` does not
exist in the tree today; `$PID` is what main kept (`tools/test.ps1:424`,
`tools/graphics_tests.ps1:58-59`). Worth remembering next time two worktrees fix the same
observed symptom the same night: they can converge on two different, both-defensible keys, and
only one survives the merge.

**The dev-save gear loss was attributed to the capture test, and that attribution was
incomplete.** Diagnosing it that way explained why a screenshot-capture run left a dev save
missing its equipment, but `RunManager.EndRun` never checking `hasRun` (see the FIXED table
above, `95c0b8b3`) is a second, independent way to reach the exact same symptom: hub -> Main
Menu, and both Exit doors, stripped every character's gear and the stockpile whether or not a
run was actually active, on every ordinary play session that took that path -- not just under a
test harness. `RunEndingTests` now covers the general case; the capture-test-specific fix from
earlier in the week covered only the specific path that first surfaced it.
