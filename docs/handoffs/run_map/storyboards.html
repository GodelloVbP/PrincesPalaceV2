What changed

Anchors stand with the boss

Beat an elite on the map and it is gone. Leave it alive and it fights beside the boss. This replaces the abstract wards, so no new combat system is needed. The enemy stage has three slots, so the cap is two anchors per realm.

Restraint pays

Walking the spine past a pocket heals 8%. Bypassing becomes a choice with a reward, so "always pocket when healthy" stops being free.

A time budget

One skippable opening pan, at most 2.5 s, from the heart up to the party. Walks stay capped.

An honest pitch

A visible boss is standard. The pitch is that the boss you face is the one your route left behind.

Storyboards

Greybox frames at 16:9: shapes and placement, not final art. Medallions: circle = fight, hexagon = anchor elite, square = shop, rounded square = event, triangle = rest, diamond = treasure, chest = cache.

FRAME 1 OF 5

Arriving in a realm

The camera opens on the heart at the bottom: the Forest Troll, with the Elder Treant and the Ironback Beetle standing beside it. Then it rises to the party at the top. The whole realm is visible: the spine, a fork (shop or fight), and two pockets that each hold an anchor. Dotted red roots tie each anchor to the heart.

The player learns the goal before taking a step: which monsters will stand with the boss, and where they can be reached.

FRAME 2 OF 5

Choosing a pocket

Close-up at a pocket's mouth. The card compares the two open choices. Spine: two Giant Rats, strength 2, gold plus a consumable, and +8% HP for walking past the pocket. Pocket: the Elder Treant, strength 3, a spell book with treasure behind it, two extra rooms, and if left alive it stands with the boss.

Prize and HP against restraint and breath. The answer depends on the party's HP and build, which is the decision the spec asks for.

FRAME 3 OF 5

An anchor falls

Back on the map after the Treant fight. Its node withers, its root dies back toward the heart, and warm light spreads around the cleared ground. The spine fight the party skipped is greyed out. An inset shows the heart far below, with the Treant's place beside it empty.

The world responds in two places at once: the realm eases where the party fought, and the boss visibly loses an ally. Nothing on screen counts or labels it.

FRAME 4 OF 5

The heart

The bottom of the realm. The Forest Troll waits with the Ironback Beetle, the anchor this party left alive, still at its side, its root running back up to the pocket the party skipped. The card says it plainly: the fight is Troll plus Beetle, and the drop gains one reward for the Beetle.

The route the party chose has built this fight. Players feel the consequence before they commit to it.

FRAME 5 OF 5

The same realm, run 12

Same realm, later run. The walked route glows, the dead Treant's node stays withered, and a warm dotted thread now runs through the whole realm. The thread is a recurring motif, seen in every realm and clearer each run, gated by meta-progression. It never explains itself.

The subtext layer: an emotional motif rather than a real-life object, which the bible rules out. Early runs show almost none of it.

What to judge

Your spec's two tests. Surface: does frame 1 read as "a strange realm full of monsters", not a board game? (In greybox, no. The test only means something with the painted realm and real monster portraits on the medallions. Treat that as the open risk.) Thoroughness: does frame 2 feel like a decision you'd hesitate over?
