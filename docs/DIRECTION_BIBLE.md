# What I think the game is

Status: draft 2 2026-09-26, awaiting owner corrections. Live copy: https://claude.ai/artifact/JWHFGAqwZTycdfKUhwYHBY

*Prince's Palace v2 · direction bible · draft 2 · 2026-09-26*

This is my current understanding, one statement per line, so each can be corrected on its own. The tag on each line says where it came from, which also shows how much weight it carries.

Key: **[You]** you said it · **[My read]** my inference, the most likely to be wrong · **[Ruled out]** you rejected it · **[Open]** not decided.

## 1 · The truth under the game

- **[You]** Prince is a cat. His human "owner" is, in Prince's eyes, his preferred servant.
- **[You]** The owner is depressed and has PTSD.
- **[You]** Everything the player sees is the owner's imagination and emotions: his demons and his trauma given form.
- **[You]** Prince pulls the souls of deceased animals into his palace and sends them to fight those demons, out of love for his owner.
- **[You]** That isn't right by ethical standards. The souls have no stake in the owner's troubles, and Prince uses them for his own ends. The grey lives here.
- **[You]** Prince is fully aware of what he asks of them, and he has his own priorities and agenda.
- **[You]** Clearing the world does help the owner.
- **[You]** The player is one of the souls, knows nothing of this, and can slowly unravel it.
- **[You]** The trauma is defined by the author and shown vaguely in play.
- **[You]** The owner can appear in events that are his memories. The Petting Zoo is one.
- **[You]** In the end it's a game. Playable elements come first, and any of this can change when gameplay needs it.

## 2 · Tone

- **[My read]** Melancholic and tender more than grim. It's sad because someone is suffering, not because it's dark for its own sake.
- **[My read]** Prince carries the lightness: a cat's entitlement and certainty, played straight. The humour is his, and it sits uneasily next to what he's doing to the souls.
- **[My read]** Uncanny rather than horrifying. The world feels wrong in ways that are emotional, not gory.
- **[You]** Not good versus bad. Nuanced and grey.

## 3 · The world

- **[You]** It stays fantasy, because it is his imagination and emotions.
- **[Ruled out]** Correlating the world to real-life objects: the armchair-cliff, the window-moon.
- **[My read]** Each realm is an emotional landscape: a feeling or state of mind rendered as a fantasy place. Numbness might be a frozen, soundless expanse; dread an endless dusk; guilt a place that keeps rebuilding what you break. The link to the owner is emotional, never literal.
- **[My read]** The monsters are his demons as fantasy creatures: fears, intrusive memories, self-loathing, exhaustion. Their look and behaviour should echo the feeling they stand for, even if the player can't name it.
- **[My read]** Going deeper means going further into him. The early realms are near the surface, and the deepest is closest to the trauma, which is never shown plainly.
- **[My read]** Clearing a realm eases it: light returns, the weather calms, colour comes back, the sound softens. That's the emotional equivalent of him feeling a little better, and it's never labelled.
- **[My read]** The unravelling comes from emotional motifs that recur across realms and runs, not from objects. A particular colour, a melody, a phrase, a shape that keeps returning. More of it becomes visible as meta-progression advances.
- **[Open]** Whether today's enemies (the Giant Rat, the beetle, the treant) fit "demons as feelings", or read as generic dungeon fauna.

## 4 · The characters

- **[You]** Shawn (a sheep, "he"), Odette (an owl) and Bjorn (a bear) are the souls. The other animals in the files are placeholders.
- **[You]** The souls have no connection to the owner.
- **[My read]** Each soul had its own life and its own death, which the player may learn about. That's a second thing to unravel, separate from the owner's.
- **[My read]** The palace is where the souls are pulled *to*. It is the hub, and it's Prince's domain.

## 5 · The run map

- **[You]** No hidden "?" rooms.
- **[You]** Show what a fight rewards before you pick it ("probably a good idea").
- **[You]** No time-pressure or pursuit mechanic.
- **[You]** Legs may vary in length, or at least get longer.
- **[You]** Elite placement: forced or avoidable, left open.
- **[Ruled out]** Reality lanes, the contract board, dens in a painted place, and framing the map as Prince's view of his palace.
- **[You]** The prototypes so far all feel the same. The design has to be rethought, not given gimmicks.
- **[My read]** The map is the souls' view: a realm they've been sent into without explanation.
- **[My read]** The core decision between fights is how much risk to put the party through, given its HP, gold and build, and for which reward.
- **[My read]** A screenshot of the map must read as "a strange fantasy realm full of monsters" to someone who has never played.
- **[My read]** A leg is one realm, and each leg goes deeper.
- **[Open]** What the map screen actually is. It should come from the fiction above, not from genre habit.

## 6 · Look

- **[You]** A Hades-style painterly art direction.
- **[You]** Enemy sprites follow the Giant Rat sheet: flat cel shading with bold outlines.
- **[You]** No hover scaling anywhere in the UI.
- **[My read]** Each realm has one dominant emotional colour, and clearing it shifts that colour.

## 7 · Never

- **[You]** A good-versus-evil framing.
- **[My read]** Telling the player outright that they're healing someone. No mood meter or sanity bar for the owner.
- **[My read]** Clinical or literal imagery: hospitals, pills, therapy.
- **[My read]** Presenting Prince as simply good or simply bad.
